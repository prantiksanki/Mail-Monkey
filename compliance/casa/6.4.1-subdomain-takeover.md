# CASA 6.4.1 — Configuration: Protect against subdomain takeover

> **Control 6.4.1 — Configuration / Protect application from subdomain
> takeover**
>
> *The application shall not be susceptible to subdomain takeovers.*
>
> **Deliverable**
> 1. Provide evidence of DNS configuration for the target domain and
>    subdomains, confirming that either all subdomains are explicitly defined
>    and point to IP addresses or other domains controlled by your
>    organization; or where the record points to a third party owned domain,
>    confirm that the subdomain record isn't configured to point to a
>    non-existing or non-active resource/external service/endpoint.

---

## What subdomain takeover is

A DNS record (typically a CNAME) points a subdomain at a third-party service
(a cloud host, a SaaS platform, a static-site host). If that third-party
resource is later deprovisioned or deleted without also removing the DNS
record, the record becomes "dangling" — pointing at a resource that no
longer exists. An attacker can then claim that same resource on the
third-party service and have it start responding for the victim's subdomain,
since DNS still resolves there. This lets the attacker serve arbitrary
content (including phishing pages) under a trusted domain.

---

## Short answer (for the CASA portal)

A code review found this application has **no dynamic subdomain-per-tenant
or custom-domain feature** — every route is path-based
(`/u/{username}`, not `{username}.domain.com`), and no code extracts a
subdomain from the request host to route to tenant-specific content. This
means subdomain takeover carries no application-level risk factor here; it is
purely a DNS-zone hygiene question, answered by confirming the actual DNS
records for the hostnames the application references. Those hostnames are
enumerated below for direct verification against the live DNS zone.

---

## Deliverable — code-level review and hostname inventory

### No subdomain-per-tenant or custom-domain feature exists

The creator-profile / "My Page" feature — the only place a per-user public
URL exists — builds its URL as `${getBaseUrl()}/u/${username}`
(`apps/web/app/(marketing)/[locale]/u/[username]/page.tsx`): a **path**, not
a host. No code anywhere in `apps/web` or `packages/api` extracts a
subdomain from the `Host` header for routing purposes, and no feature lets a
user or organization bind their own domain/subdomain to this application
(which would carry a *different*, domain-ownership-verification risk on
their end, not applicable here since no such feature exists). `proxy.ts`
branches only on `pathname`, never on host-derived tenant identity.
