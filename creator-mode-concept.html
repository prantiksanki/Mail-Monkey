01 — The Problem

Sharing your email doesn't work well today.

Creators and freelancers do this all the time: they put their email in their Instagram bio, their YouTube "About" page, or their portfolio site. It's one of the most common things people do online. But when someone taps that email, they land on a blank, empty mailto: screen. No photo, no info, no way to know if you're even free to chat this week.

＠What happens now

Tapping an email just opens a blank message box. No branding, no bio, nothing to show who you are or why someone should reach out.

↻What it costs you

Good opportunities — brand deals, client jobs, collab offers — get typed into a cold, empty message and often never get sent at all.

Linktree gives you one link for everything. Calendly lets people book time with you. But neither one is an inbox — so creators end up using three different tools and still have to go back to email anyway. ZeroMail is already an inbox. We just need to add a public page on top of it.

03 — The Journey

From Instagram bio to inbox, in five steps.

Here's the whole idea in one simple flow. The visitor never has to sign up for anything.

1
Creator

Turns on Creator or Freelancer mode

Just one switch in account settings. They pick a name for their page, write a short bio, add links, upload a photo, and set the times they're free.

2
Creator

Shares their page link, not their raw email

zeromail.com/@mayaedits goes in the Instagram bio, YouTube about page, or website footer — instead of a plain email address.

3
Visitor

Sees a real page, not a blank form

They see the creator's bio, links, work or services, and — if turned on — the times they're free. It all looks like the creator's own style.

4
Visitor

Sends a message or books a time — no account needed

They fill in a short form (name, what it's about, budget) or pick a time on the calendar. Just one step, no app to download.

5
Creator

It shows up as a real email, already sorted

The message lands in the creator's normal ZeroMail inbox, already labeled "Profile inquiry," ready for the same tools and AI help every other email gets.

04 — What We'd Build

Three main pieces.

Piece One

Public Profile Page

A page at the creator's own link: bio, photo, links, and a list of services or a media kit. This is the front door — easy to find, easy to share, and quick to load even if it gets a big spike of visits from one Instagram story.

Piece Two

Public Calendar

Not the same as the private calendar already in the app. This is a separate, public view that only shows the times the creator chooses to open up — a stranger never sees the real, private calendar underneath.

Piece Three

Messages Go Straight to the Inbox

Every message or booking becomes a normal email in the inbox — not a separate tool to check. It gets the same rules, AI help, and reminders as any other email, so there's still just one place to keep track of everything.

05 — How This Fits Into ZeroMail Today

We already have a head start.

Most of the pieces we'd need already exist in some form in the app. This isn't starting from zero.

User.username Already there · not used for a public page yet

Every account already has its own username. We can use that same name for /@yourname — no need to build a new naming system, just a new public page that shows it.

Contact form tool Already built · needs a public version

We already have a way for someone to fill out a form and have the message reach a user. A profile inquiry works the same way, just pointed at one specific person's inbox.

Calendar feature Private only right now · booking is new work

The calendar we have today only shows a signed-in user their own Google Calendar. There's no public or bookable version yet — that part would be new work.

Paid plans Two plans today · room for a third

Billing already has a free plan and a paid plan, each with its own limits. Creator and Freelancer features can be added as a new plan, without changing how billing works.

Public pages Already done elsewhere in the app

The app already has public pages that don't need a login. A profile page would work the same way — nothing new to invent there.

06 — How We'd Build It

Four steps. Each one works on its own.

Step01

Simple profile page first

Add the account type switch, let people claim their page name, and build a public bio/links page. No calendar or stats yet — first check that people actually share and use it.

  • Account type field and settings switch
  • Public profile page
  • Contact form that sends to the inbox
Step02

Add the bookable calendar

The public calendar view: creators set the times they're free each week, visitors pick a time, and both people get a confirmation.

  • Free/busy times kept separate from the private calendar
  • Booking confirmations, plus reschedule and cancel
Step03

Add paid features and more detail

Paid booking slots for freelancers, media kits for creators, page-visit stats, and the ability to customize how the page looks.

  • Paid booking slots (freelancer plan)
  • Page visits and inquiry stats
  • Custom colors, layout, and domain
Step04

Help people find each other

An optional directory where people can browse creators by category, so pages start bringing in visitors on their own, not just from shared links.

  • Public directory with category filters
  • "People like this" suggestions

08 — Things to Decide First

Questions to answer before we start building.

?

Spam on the public contact form

Since anyone can send a message without logging in, we need spam limits, bot blocking, and a way to report or block someone, right from the start.

?

People taking names that aren't theirs

Since username can already be picked by anyone, we need a simple way to check or dispute a name, especially for well-known people.

?

Keeping the real calendar private

The public calendar must only ever show "free" or "busy" during the hours the creator has chosen to open up — never any real event details from their private calendar.

?

How we price it

Should Creator/Freelancer mode be a paid plan, a free feature that gets people to later pay for more inbox features, or both? This affects how hard we push it at launch.

09 — Next Step
